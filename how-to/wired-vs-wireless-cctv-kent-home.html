Wired PoE or wireless: choosing a camera system for a Kent home

Both wired PoE and wireless camera systems do the job well. The right choice comes down to your property, not a general rule, here is how to actually decide.

What wireless gets you

Battery or solar-powered cameras that talk to a base station or directly to your Wi-Fi. No cable runs, no chasing walls, and a camera can go almost anywhere with a decent signal and a bit of sky for a solar panel. Installation is fast and non-disruptive, which suits rented properties, listed buildings, or anyone who does not want cable clipped along a wall.

The trade-off is dependency on two things staying reliable: battery life (or solar charging) and Wi-Fi signal. Both are checkable and manageable, but they are ongoing considerations rather than a one-off install.

What wired PoE gets you

A single cable carries both power and data to each camera, run back to a network video recorder (NVR). No batteries to charge, no Wi-Fi drop-outs, and footage is recorded locally to a device you control rather than relying on a cloud subscription. For a shop, an office, or a larger home with several cameras, this is usually the more reliable long-term setup.

The trade-off is the install itself: cable needs to be run, ideally through existing routes (loft space, ducting, cavities) rather than surface-clipped everywhere, which takes longer and costs more upfront than a wireless camera you can be using the same afternoon.

Questions worth answering before you choose

  • How many cameras, realistically? One or two on a modern house, wireless is usually the simpler and cheaper route. Four or more on a property that wants permanent, reliable coverage, wired starts to make more sense.
  • What are your walls made of? Solid flint, stone or thick period brick (common through Sandwich's older streets) attenuates Wi-Fi more than modern stud walls, which pushes the case towards wired, or at least a mesh point, for anything beyond the front door.
  • Is the property rented, listed, or in a conservation area? Wireless avoids cable runs and fixings that a landlord or conservation officer might object to. Wired needs a proper conversation about routes and any consent needed first.
  • Do you want footage stored locally or in the cloud? Wired PoE with an NVR keeps footage on a device at the property. Wireless systems usually rely on a cloud subscription, worth checking what that costs long-term, not just the hardware price.

A sensible default for most East Kent homes

For most houses, one to four cameras, wireless (or solar for anything off the beaten track, a shed or a boundary fence) covers it well and keeps disruption low. For a shop, office, larger property, or anywhere wanting a permanent local recording and multiple cameras, wired PoE earns its extra install time.

Plenty of properties end up with a mix: wireless on the areas that do not need a cable run, wired where a shop front or a main entrance genuinely benefits from a rock-solid connection. That is a perfectly sensible outcome, not a compromise.
