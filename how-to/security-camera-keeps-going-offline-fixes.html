Security camera keeps going offline: how to fix it

A camera that keeps going offline usually has one of four problems: no power, a weak signal at its position, the wrong Wi-Fi band, or a router that changed. Check them in that order, starting with the light on the camera and a phone test at the camera's spot.

Start with power

Look at the camera. Does it have a status light? Eufy's offline guide for one of its outdoor cameras starts by checking for a solid blue light and, if there is none, confirming the original adapter and cable are in use and trying a different socket. For battery cameras, check the battery level in the app, and for solar cameras check that the panel is working. A flat battery looks exactly like a Wi-Fi fault.

Was it a router change?

If the camera worked yesterday and not today, ask whether anything changed: a new router from your broadband provider, a new Wi-Fi name or password, a reset after a power cut, or a switch to a different channel. A camera remembers the old details. If the network details changed, the camera usually needs to be told the new ones through the app, and some need a re-add. Our extender and mesh guide notes the same trap.

Check the Wi-Fi band

Ring states that all of its doorbells and cameras work on 2.4 GHz, and that a device on 2.4 GHz can drop offline after being moved to a 5 GHz-only network. Eufy states its devices support 2.4 GHz only. If your router combines both bands under one name, the camera should still pick 2.4 GHz, but if it will not, splitting the names helps. Make sure the 2.4 GHz band is switched on at all.

Measure the signal where the camera is

Stand next to the camera with your phone on the 2.4 GHz network and see if it connects and loads pages without trouble. Eufy suggests exactly that test, and also tells you to check the Wi-Fi reading in the camera settings, where it should say "Great". If it is weak, moving the router closer, adding a mesh unit or fixing a poor position is the answer. Repeat in the evening, when more neighbouring networks are busy.

Look for interference and obstacles

New things near the camera or router can disrupt a signal: a metal gate, a new shed, a large mirror, a baby monitor or an extension of the house. If the fault began when something was built or moved, that is your lead.

Try a phone hotspot test

Eufy's guide suggests connecting the camera to a phone hotspot. If it connects there but not to your home network, the camera is probably fine and the problem is the home network or signal. If it will not connect anywhere, suspect the camera or its power.

Re-add or reset as a last step

  1. Save any recordings you want to keep.
  2. Remove the camera from the app and add it again.
  3. If that fails, use the factory reset described in your camera's guide. For that Eufy camera it is a 10-second press of the SYNC button, but the method varies by model, so check yours.
  4. If it still fails, contact the manufacturer with the serial number.

Battery and solar cameras

A battery camera that has run flat will show offline long before anyone thinks to look at the charge. Check the level in the app, and if the camera is on a solar panel, look at whether the panel is clean, unshaded and set as the power source. Winter is the usual time for this to bite, and our guide to solar panels in a UK winter covers the planning. A camera that has dropped offline just once after a power cut is different: the router may have come back with a delay, so restart the camera before you start changing settings.

Why it may not be the camera at all

Intermittent dropouts usually point to the network, not the device. If the same camera is fine in one spot and offline in another, the cure is signal. If several cameras drop at once, look at the router. Sandwich's thick brick and flint walls and long gardens make weak signal common, which is why we survey the signal at each camera position.

Checklist

  • Power or battery confirmed
  • Router, name and password unchanged since it last worked
  • 2.4 GHz band on
  • Phone test at the camera position
  • Camera settings show a good Wi-Fi reading
  • Re-add, then reset, only after the above

Common questions

Why does my security camera keep going offline?

The usual causes are no power or a flat battery, a weak Wi-Fi signal at the camera, the wrong Wi-Fi band, or a changed router name or password. Check them in that order.

Does my camera need 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz?

Ring states its devices work on 2.4 GHz, and Eufy states its devices support 2.4 GHz only. Some newer models may differ, so check your model's guide.

Do I have to re-add the camera after changing my router?

Often you need to update the Wi-Fi details in the app, and sometimes re-add the camera. The steps depend on the brand and model.

When should I factory reset a camera?

Only after checking power, band and signal, and after saving any recordings you want. The reset method varies by model, so follow your camera's guide.
