Video doorbell placement: fewer false alerts, less of the street

A doorbell camera mounted without much thought does two things badly: it buries you in alerts every time someone walks past, and it can catch far more of the street or a neighbour's property than you realise. Both are fixable at install.

Why false alerts happen

Most video doorbells trigger on motion within a set distance, often several metres by default. Mounted at a standard door height facing a footpath, pavement or a road, that range easily catches every passer-by, cyclist and car, which is exactly why so many doorbell owners end up muting notifications altogether within a few weeks.

The fix is usually in the settings, not the hardware: narrowing the motion zone to your own path and porch, adjusting sensitivity, and in some cases physically angling the unit a few degrees away from the road. A ten-minute adjustment at install saves months of notification fatigue.

Positioning for a coastal footpath or a busy street

Properties near Sandwich Bay's footpaths, or along a village high street like Wingham's, tend to get the worst of both problems: high foot traffic for false alerts, and a genuine privacy question about how much of a public path the camera captures. Angling the doorbell down and towards your own door, rather than straight out at head height, usually solves both at once, you lose a bit of peripheral view but gain a camera that is actually useful.

If your property sits close to a neighbour's window, garden or shared path, the same logic applies there too, angle towards your own entrance rather than sweeping across their boundary.

The privacy point, briefly

A doorbell that only captures your own porch and immediate approach is exempt from most data protection obligations under the household exemption in UK law. The moment it also captures a neighbour's garden, a shared path, or the public street as a matter of course, that exemption narrows and some duties can apply, being able to explain why, storing footage sensibly, and putting up signage in some cases. This is genuinely useful to get right at install rather than after a neighbour raises it. Our UK CCTV & doorbell law guide covers this in full, including the audio question, which matters more than most people expect.

A short placement checklist

  • Mount at a height and angle that favours your own porch over the wider street
  • Set the motion zone to your path and door, not the full detection range
  • Check the audio recording range separately from the video frame, many devices pick up sound well beyond what the camera shows
  • Test for a week and adjust sensitivity if you are still getting frequent car or pedestrian alerts
  • If your view genuinely has to include a shared path or footpath, consider a privacy mask if your app supports it
